Design proposal  ·  Roosevelt Island Chess Club  ·  Oct 15, 2026

Every game starts with one move.

The new site tells its story with a real 3D chess game that plays as you scroll. Scroll down and the pieces move. Scroll up and they move back. Each part of the game opens a part of the club: why chess, the classes, the coaches, club life, and finally the call to book a $30 trial class.

01 · The idea

The whole site is one famous game

We play the Opera Game from 1858, one of the most loved games in chess history. It lasts just 17 moves and has everything a parent wants their child to learn: develop your pieces, work as a team, be brave, finish clean. The moves themselves are public history, so nobody owns them.

This fits the club's own words, "Learn. Think. Play. Grow." and "Your first move starts here." A child starts at move one, not knowing how the knight jumps, and ends at checkmate. The page does the same.

02 · Hero concepts

Three directions, one recommended

Recommended

A · The Opera Game

A handcrafted board floats in a dusk-lit room. As you scroll, the camera glides around it and the 17 moves play out. Captions land on the big moments. At checkmate the board settles, the losing king tips over, and the 3D club medallion rises into view with the booking button.

Why: it tells a real story with a clear beginning and end, and the mate is a natural place for the call to action.

Option

B · Island Board

The board is shaped like Roosevelt Island, floating on a glowing East River with the tram line overhead and Manhattan as soft light columns. Pieces march along the island to the club's front door at 524 Main St.

Trade-off: very local and memorable, but the scene is heavier to build and to run on phones, and the chess story is weaker.

Option

C · The Knight's Path

The camera follows the knight from the club logo as it jumps across a huge board. Each square it lands on opens one chapter. At the end it leaps into place on the medallion.

Trade-off: great close-up hero shots of one piece, but less of a story than a real game.

A and C can mix: the logo's knight can be the "hero piece" inside the Opera Game, with the camera following it at the key moments.

04 · The 3D logo

The club medallion, in 3D

We redraw the club logo as clean vector art first. That gives sharp files for print, social media and the favicon. Then we build it in 3D from those same shapes.

  • Orange enamel disc with a glossy coat that catches the light.
  • Checkerboard relief raised a few millimeters, in brushed ivory.
  • The knight fully sculpted and standing out from the face, like a real piece.
  • "Roosevelt Island" script raised in polished ivory, with "Chess Club" stamped below.
  • Rim with a fine engraved edge, like a coin.

It reacts to the visitor: it follows the mouse a little, and you can drag to spin it. It opens the story and comes back at checkmate.

05 · Premium, with no glitches

How the 3D is made to look expensive and stay solid

Materials

  • Staunton-style pieces, shaped from our own drawn profiles with smooth, high-detail curves.
  • Boxwood and ebonized walnut wood with a satin lacquer, subtle grain, and soft light on the edges.
  • A felt-and-maple board with a walnut frame, inlaid border, and engraved a–h / 1–8 coordinates.
  • Studio lighting: one warm key light, a cool river-blue rim light, and soft contact shadows under every piece.
  • Film-style color and gentle depth of field on close-ups. Applied lightly, never blurry.

Engineering against glitches

  • The scene follows the scroll position. Each scroll point always gives the same frame, so fast scrolling, jumping, or scrolling back can't leave a piece in the wrong place.
  • Smooth easing that matches any screen refresh rate (60, 120, 144 Hz).
  • Rendering stops when idle or when the scene is off screen, which saves battery and keeps phones cool.
  • Automatic quality levels: full effects on strong computers, a lighter version on phones, and a still image when 3D isn't available.
  • Nothing jumps on load: the first frame is pre-rendered and swaps to live 3D without a visible flash.
  • Testing on Chrome, Safari, Firefox, iPhone and Android before anyone sees it.

06 · Interactive moments

Things the visitor does, not just watches

Drag to look around. At any pause in the story, drag the board to orbit it. It glides back when you scroll again.

"Your move" puzzle. Halfway down, a mate-in-one on the 3D board. Click a piece, click its square. Solve it and the board celebrates.

Spin the medallion. The 3D logo tilts toward the mouse and spins when dragged.

Hover a coach. Each coach card shows their title piece in 3D: the GM's king, the FM's queen, and so on.

09 · Phones, access, speed

Works for every parent on every device

Phones get real 3D too. A lighter scene with the same story. Unlike a video hero, nothing big has to download.

Reduced motion respected. Visitors who turn off animation get still, composed frames of each chapter.

Readable without 3D. All text is real page text, so search engines and screen readers get every word.

Fast. Target under 2.5 s to first view on 4G. The 3D engine loads after the words are already on screen.

11 · Hosting

Cloudflare Pages, connected to GitHub

The site is plain files, so hosting is free on Cloudflare Pages. The code lives in a GitHub repository. Every change pushed there goes live in about a minute, and each draft gets its own preview link to check before it goes public.

The domain rooseveltislandchessclub.com moves from WordPress.com to Cloudflare at launch. Gmail is not affected. We keep the WordPress site running until the new one is checked live, so there's no downtime.
