Our mission

To build strategic minds through the game of chess.

  • Focus

    Each class asks for a full hour of attention on one board.

  • Patience

    Players learn to slow down and think before they move.

  • Decisions

    Every move is a choice, and reviewing games shows what worked.

  • Confidence

    Every game ends with a handshake, win or lose.

Coaches

Meet the coaches.

Titles like Grandmaster and FIDE Master are awarded by FIDE, the world chess federation. All four coaches teach kids.

Grandmaster

Kazybek Nogerbek

Grandmaster since 2024 and the 2024 World Junior Champion (Under 20), scoring 8.5 out of 11. He was also the 2022 World Youth Under 18 Rapid Champion, and started playing at six.

FIDE Master

Ellan Asuela

FIDE Master from the Philippines with a peak rating of 2312. He won the Kasparov Chess Foundation Asia-Pacific Absolute Challenge in Singapore, finishing ahead of several International Masters and Grandmasters.

National Master

Michael Ocido

US National Master and coach based in New York City. He teaches clear, step-by-step lessons for kids and beginners.

FIDE Arbiter

Leo Romitman

FIDE National Arbiter (a certified tournament referee), trainer and organizer from the Philippines. He helps kids and beginners learn the basics and play with confidence.

Questions

What parents ask us.

My child has never played. Is that OK?

Yes. Many students start by learning how the pieces move, and our beginner groups start from the very first move.

How do you choose my child's group?

Coaches group students by age, experience and chess level whenever possible, so every child is challenged. The trial class is where we work this out together.

What happens in the $30 trial class?

Your child joins a class. A coach gets to know them, checks their current level and recommends the best group. Then you decide.

What do the classes cover?

Everything from how the pieces move to how to win a game. That includes tricks and traps, planning, checkmates, endgames, puzzles and practice games. Coaches also go over students' games with them and help kids who want to compete get ready for tournaments.

Are there tournaments?

Yes. The Sunday semester includes a practice tournament, the club runs fast-paced blitz tournaments and workshops, and coaches can point families to outside tournaments that suit their child's level.

How long is a class, and what does it cost?

Every class is 60 minutes. Weekly classes are $175 a month. The Sunday beginners semester is $350 for 10 classes. The trial class is $30.
